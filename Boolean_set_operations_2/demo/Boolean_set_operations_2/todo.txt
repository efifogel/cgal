1. Algebraic curves
- The family is enabled when Lexy is found (find_package(lexy); set lexy_DIR to <prefix>/lib/cmake/lexy of an installed
  Lexy); otherwise its button stays disabled. Linking foonathan::lexy::core compiles the demo as C++20 when the compiler
  supports it, unless Lexy was configured with LEXY_FORCE_CPP17. The grammar is in Polynomial_parser.h.
- The Equation button (in the curve tool bar) adds the region the curve encloses: the union of the bounded faces of
  the arrangement of the curve (Algebraic_polygons.cpp). A curve that encloses nothing (e.g., a parabola) is refused.
  Other ways to choose the region (e.g., p <= 0, which may be unbounded) could be offered.
- Library: General_polygon_set_2 over Arr_algebraic_segment_traits_2, whose sides are open, uses the unbounded planar
  topology, where union, symmetric difference, and complement come out empty, and the precondition that validates the
  polygons fails in Arr_walk_along_line_point_location (number_of_outer_ccbs() == 1). The demo derives Algebraic_traits
  with oblivious sides, so the sets use the bounded planar topology.
- The arcs are drawn through 65 points each (denser near the endpoints), computed once per arc and cached
  (algebraic_arc_points()).
- Not yet: files (.aps; a set after operations is not described by equations alone, so arcs would need their curve,
  arc number, and endpoints), Python programs (the log has a comment instead), and the console.
- Syntax errors report the first error only, with its column; semantic errors (a divisor or an exponent that is not a
  constant) have no column.

2. Polycurves
- Only Segments can be the subcurve family of Polyline/Polycurve; the other family buttons are disabled in polycurve
  mode. Next: polycurves of circle segments (.pcps), conics (.pqps), and Bezier curves (.pbps); then nested ones
  (.pplps, ...). The suffix scheme is documented in Polygon_io.h.
- The boundary of a polyline polygon is subdivided into maximal x-monotone polylines (Polyline_boundary.h); the
  endpoints of the polylines are the vertices of the polygon (filled marks) and the other vertices are vertices of the
  polylines (hollow marks), also while the boundary is constructed. Other ways to let the user choose the vertices of
  the polygon (e.g., a modifier key while clicking) would need the .plps format to mark them, since now it holds only
  the vertices and the reader subdivides again.
- The results of operations keep the polylines of the operands, except where they are split at intersections.
- The marks are painted in pixels around the vertices, so the bounding rectangle of the item is extended by their
  extent, converted to scene units at the current scale of the view (marks_margin()). This relies on the scene having
  no index (NoIndex), since the margin changes with zooming without a call to prepareGeometryChange().

3. Conic polygons
- Files (.qps) and programs describe an arc by its conic, its orientation, and its endpoints, each approximated and
  given with an auxiliary conic through it (Conic_arcs.h). The auxiliary conic of an endpoint is the conic of the
  neighboring arc; consecutive arcs of the same conic are merged first. Where the conics of two neighbors coincide
  anyway (different orientations), the line through the points of vertical tangency is used, which is correct only at
  such points.
- A segment must be constructed with the equation of its line (r = s = t = 0); a segment constructed from its
  endpoints only has no coefficients and cannot be written (saving reports it). The conic input now constructs its
  segments with their equations.
- data/conic has samples: a lens, a half disk, a D shape (a segment between irrational points), an ellipse, and the
  union of two ellipses. Saving a loaded file writes the same arcs, possibly starting at another arc and with negated
  coefficients.

4. Python programs
- The circle-segment family logs its drawn polygons from the points the user clicked (Circular_source.h), and the conic
  family from the arc descriptions; the programs import CGALPY_kerEpec_aos2Cs_bso2 and CGALPY_kerEpec_aos2Conic_bso2.
  Neither program was run, because the bindings are not built yet. Check:
  - Integer is constructed from a Python int only (init_implicit<int>), so coefficients beyond 32 bits fail;
  - squared_distance() (defined in the program) relies on the arithmetic of Ker.FT;
  - Traits.Point_2 of the conic traits is constructed from floats (Algebraic from double);
  - Result.COLLINEAR (for segments) exists;
  - polygon() filters the results of make_x_monotone with isinstance(o, X_monotone_curve_2).
- Polygons read from files are not logged, in any family, so a program misses them.
- The Bezier and polyline families are not logged; the console supports linear polygons only; the Minkowski sum is
  not logged.

5. Small items
- The writers of .lps, .plps, .cps, and .bps use the default precision of the stream (6 digits), which rounds the
  coordinates; use the precision of a double (17 digits), as the .qps writer does.
- read_polyline_boundary() ignores the DOUBLE header (as read_linear_boundary() does).
- The polycurve tool bar pushes the Operations panel down; in a small window the bucket rows scroll. The empty curve
  tool bar (for families without tools) keeps its height.
- The message that switches the family uses the internal names ("linear polygons"), not the labels ("Segments").
- help/about_demo.html is out of date (e.g., "Insert Linear polygon").
- The GUI regression scripts (drawing, undo, files, console) are not in the repository; their references predate the
  new tool bar and the polyline marks.
